# Make Every Dent Tell a Story

A demolition derby game feels authentic when damage is **cumulative, zone-based and tied to the car's systems**. Totalling the whole structure is not how real derby cars die. Drivers report about **52 collisions per event at a mean 26 mph (maximum 45 mph), 55% of them rear-end** ([Weill Cornell VIVO](https://vivo.weill.cornell.edu/display/pubid15827919)). Cars go out when a specific system fails: a punctured radiator, bent tie rods, wheels jammed against crushed fenders, or a driveshaft that falls out of the transmission. Shipped games have mostly settled on a layered recipe: a deformable shell, hinged panels that can tear off, per-zone health feeding mechanical penalties, shader wear masks and particles. Full soft-body simulation is the exception. BeamNG steps physics at 2,000 Hz, a cost that rules out a 20-car field on consumer hardware. Real derbies also hand the design team a ready-made ruleset. The last car moving wins. Each car must make an aggressive hit about every 60 seconds. Hitting a driver's door is forbidden, hiding ("sandbagging") is banned, and a second fire disqualifies. Events run as heats, then a consolation round, then a feature, across classes that go from stock to full build. The paint culture is loud, cheap, spray-can folk art, framed by rulebook-mandated numbers of 18 inches or more on doors and roof and a contrasting driver's door. Families and school classes often do the painting, and "best appearing" awards reward it. Using fictional cars is a strategic advantage: licensed racing games negotiate damage levels with each manufacturer, so a non-licensed derby game can wreck its cars as hard as the fun requires.

## Derby cars die from broken systems, not crushed bodies

### Stiff frames and soft crush zones give two damage personalities

Modern road cars are built as a stiff occupant "safety cage" surrounded by engineered crush zones. The crush zones deform to lengthen the stopping time and absorb energy, and engineers judge a crash by how far the structure intrudes into the cabin ([IIHS](https://www.iihs.org/ratings/about-our-tests); [Motor1](https://www.motor1.com/news/814631/why-cars-are-designed-to-crumple-in-a-crash/)). Crush zones have a ceiling. When IIHS-linked tests pushed impact speed past design levels, the excess energy went into the occupant compartment and injury measures rose ([IIHS](https://www.iihs.org/topics/bibliography/ref/2218)). For a game, that suggests a **nonlinear, threshold-based damage model**: below the threshold, energy goes into cosmetic crumple; above it, energy spills into core systems.

Derby cars are mostly the opposite of modern unibodies. Competitors have traditionally used **full-size American body-on-frame sedans and wagons, especially from the 1960s and 70s**, whose separate ladder frames resist deformation ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_derby); [CarFromJapan](https://carfromjapan.com/article/body-on-frame-vs-unibody-construction)). Collision-repair trades classify frame damage as **mash** (shortened like an accordion), **sway** (bent sideways), **sag** (center drops), **diamond** (one rail pushed back, so the car "dog-tracks" crabwise) and **twist** (one corner high or low), and these often occur together ([FenderBender](https://www.fenderbender.com/running-a-shop/operations/article/33019960/advanced-structural-analysis); [Cash Auto Salvage](https://www.cashautosalvage.com/frame-damage/)). Diamond or twist is a fairly simple repair on a full frame, but it makes a unibody unrepairable ([FenderBender](https://www.fenderbender.com/running-a-shop/operations/article/33020417/entering-a-new-dimension)). That gives a game two damage personalities. A body-on-frame car shows lots of panel damage but treats its frame as a tough separate health pool, and frame damage shows up as *handling* faults: pulling, crabbing, leaning. A unibody absorbs the first big hit well, but each crush zone is effectively single-use, and frame-level damage is close to fatal.

### Impact direction sets the failure order

Frontal load runs from the bumper and crash box to the front rails, then the engine, then the firewall and toe-pan. Most of the energy is absorbed by the rails and engine within roughly **35 ms**, in a whole event of about **70–120 ms** ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S230718772300007X); [Nature Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-43474-z); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331098166_FRONTAL_CRASH_SIMULATION_OF_A_CHASSIS_FRAME)). A real NHTSA investigation recorded the bumper beam, both frame rails and the lower radiator support deforming in a single frontal crash ([NHTSA](https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/Publication/813312)). Offset corner hits behave differently. IIHS's small-overlap test loads the **wheel, suspension and hinge pillar** directly, and the wheel can be forced back into the footwell. Doors have torn open and hinge pillars have torn off in these tests, and one SUV showed **15 inches** of intrusion at the lower hinge pillar ([IIHS](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/small-overlap-gap-vehicles-with-good-driver-protection-may-leave-passengers-at-risk); [TheTruthAboutCars](https://TheTruthAboutCars.com/2018/06/two-long-tooth-midsize-suvs-fail-latest-round-crash-tests/)). In side hits the door is weaker than the B-pillar and sill around it ([IIHS](https://iihs.org/ratings/about-our-tests/side)). In rear hits the axle can be shoved forward, about 4 inches in one owner's account, jamming the driveshaft into the transmission ([JustAnswer, anecdotal](https://www.justanswer.com/chevy/rcp3s-2015-silverado-2wd-rear-ended-car.html)). Roof strength depends heavily on the bonded windshield, which can supply **over 30% of static roof strength** ([NHTSA ESV](https://www-nrd.nhtsa.dot.gov/pdf/ESV/esv17/Proceed/00006.pdf)). Derby rules strip that glass, so a derby car's roof crushes more easily than a road car's (inference).

For the game, this suggests a frontal failure sequence (synthesized from the load-path sources, not from a derby-specific study): bumper and grille, then lights and radiator support with the radiator punctured and steaming, then the hood tenting up at the latch, then fenders crushing into the tires, then the rails accordioning and the engine moving back, then firewall intrusion. Corner hits should damage that corner's suspension directly, which argues for **per-wheel suspension health**. Repeated hits on the same spot should escalate. Once a crush zone has folded it no longer absorbs energy, so the next hit transmits more force inward.

### Radiators, tie rods and driveshafts end most runs

Derby practice is unusually clear about what eliminates a car. Speedway Motors calls the radiator **"probably the most vulnerable item in a demolition derby car"** ([Speedway Motors](https://www.speedwaymotors.com/the-toolbox/demolition-derby-radiator-guide/29894)). Vendors sell heavy radiator guards against "front-end collapse", while some rulebooks ban reinforcing the radiator support ([DerbyParts](https://derbyparts.com/product/bms-radiator-protector/); [5 Flags Speedway](https://www.5flagsspeedway.com/downloads/get.aspx?i=739576)). Bent tie rods "make the engine labor and scrub power", and bent wheels "shake, drag, and slow the car" ([DerbyParts](https://derbyparts.com/how-to-make-it-to-the-feature/)). Rulebooks variously allow or ban tie-rod reinforcement, and some allow fixing broken tie rods or axles between heats ([Summit County Fair](http://www.summitcountyfair.org/demolition-derby-rules-and-regulations-2015); [MAP Motorsports](https://mapmotorsports.com/rules-2)). MotoIQ's own derby car finally stopped when **"the driveshaft [fell] out of the transmission"** ([MotoIQ](https://motoiq.com/demolition-derby-tech-part-2/)). Fire is expected rather than rare. Rulebooks require leak-free fuel systems, degreased engines, an extinguisher within the driver's reach and hood holes so crews can reach the engine ([Burn Institute](https://burninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-Firefighter-Demolition-Derby-Rules.pdf)). No reliable figure exists for how long an engine survives without coolant. Estimates range from about 90 seconds to much longer, ending in a warped head, a blown gasket or a seized engine ([science.ca](https://www.science.ca/askascientist/viewquestion.php?qID=1532); [MTFCA forum](https://www.mtfca.com/phpBB3/viewtopic.php?p=131299)). For a game, that uncertainty is useful: a coolant timer of **tens of seconds** gives a steaming car a "dying but dangerous" window for one last hit.

This failure map explains derby tactics. A front-engine, rear-drive car keeps its engine, radiator, fan and steering at the front, so drivers attack trunk-first and spend "disposable" structure. In local press coverage, a rookie who led with his radiator was knocked out and said, **"Everybody says to hit from the back"**; the excerpts did not make clear which of two outlets ran the quote ([Fairborn Daily Herald](https://www.fairborndailyherald.com/?p=19165); [Farm and Dairy](https://farmanddairy.com/?p=7868)). Builders even weaken the rear on purpose so it folds *upward* and keeps the drive wheels planted. The heaviest hits are often reverse-to-reverse, with closing speeds above 50 mph ([MotoIQ](https://motoiq.com/demolition-derby-tech-part-2/)). The pattern is changing, though. Speedway Motors notes that "derbiers still hit with the rear ends, but it's not like it used to be" ([Speedway Motors](https://www.speedwaymotors.com/the-toolbox/demolition-derby-radiator-guide/29894)). Reverse ramming therefore makes a good skilled default, at the cost of driving backwards with reversed steering and poor visibility. The offensive target is an opponent's front wheel or radiator, because those map directly onto the failures that end runs.

| Real failure | Real cause | Game consequence (design guidance) |
|---|---|---|
| Radiator punctured | Frontal hit, front-end collapse | Steam, rising temperature, power loss, stall after a timer |
| Tie rod bent or broken | Front corner hit | Pull and drag; a broken rod means no steering on that side |
| Wheel tucked or tire jammed | Offset hit, crushed fender | Wheel locks or scrubs; smoke from the wheel well |
| Driveshaft out or axle broken | Heavy rear or drivetrain damage | Engine revs, car does not move: the classic "out" |
| Engine-bay fire | Fuel or oil on a hot engine | First fire put out, second fire disqualifies (see rules) |

### Campbell's linear crush model gives a defensible damage formula

Accident reconstruction offers a simple model that is still grounded in data. Campbell's 1974 SAE work found a **roughly linear relationship between impact speed and residual crush**, of the form V = b0 + b1·C. Here b0 is the speed below which nothing permanently deforms. The CRASH program recast this with stiffness coefficients A and B, so absorbed energy grows with the **square** of crush ([IRJET summary](https://issuu.com/irjet/docs/irjet-v10i391); [J.S. Held](https://jsheld.com/uploads/Vehicle-Frontal-Crush-Stiffness-Coefficients-Trends.pdf)). Those coefficients came from 1970s–90s structures, the same era as derby cars ([engrXiv](https://engrxiv.org/preprint/download/7697/12575/10872)). For the game this gives dent depth ≈ max(0, (closing speed − elastic threshold) / zone stiffness). Stiffness is set per zone (soft trunk, stiff frame horn) and jumps sharply when crush reaches a hard part such as the engine block or axle. Actual coefficient values could not be retrieved because the PDFs were blocked, so treat all numbers as tuning values. With about 52 hits per event, damage per hit should be modest and cumulative.

The visual vocabulary follows from car prep. Glass, lights and trim are already gone, and hoods are holed and chained ([Burn Institute](https://burninstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-Firefighter-Demolition-Derby-Rules.pdf); [Summit County Fair](http://www.summitcountyfair.org/demolition-derby-rules-and-regulations-2015)). What remains is crumpled sheet metal and uneven panel gaps from frame sag ([Cash Auto Salvage](https://www.cashautosalvage.com/frame-damage/)), wheels pushed back, trunks folded up, and wheels spinning far faster than the 30–40 mph cars travel ([Lewiston Tribune](https://www.lmtribune.com/northwest/demolition-derby-is-smashing-good-fun-7130a3b3)). Some common cues could not be sourced and should be checked against derby footage: white steam for the radiator, blue-grey smoke for oil, black smoke for fire, sparks from bare rims, a fan clattering against the radiator, and an engine revving with no motion.

## Shipped games layer cheap tricks instead of simulating everything

### Damage tech runs from BeamNG soft-body to arcade crumple

Car games sit on a spectrum of damage fidelity, and the derby titles cluster in the middle.

| Title | Approach | Lesson for a derby game |
|---|---|---|
| BeamNG.drive | Node-and-beam soft body at 2,000 Hz; visual mesh skinned to nodes ([BeamNG docs](https://documentation.beamng.com/beamng_tech/tools/cosimulationeditor/tight_coupling/)) | Gold standard for realism, but too CPU-heavy for a big field |
| Wreckfest / Wreckfest 2 (Bugbear, ROMU) | Soft-body chassis bending, with attached parts that detach more easily ([shapes.inc, fan](https://shapes.inc/fandom/wreckfest/deep-dive); [bsimracing](https://www.bsimracing.com/game-spotting-behind-the-scenes-of-next-car-game-car-damage-fun/)) | Closest genre match; radiator leads to overheating and a blown gasket |
| FlatOut 2 | About 40 deformable parts per car; low-integrity cars catch fire and shed wheels ([Softpedia](https://www.softpedia.com/reviews/games/playstation/FlatOut-2-55703.shtml)) | Panel count plus ragdoll spectacle |
| Burnout | Bone-driven crumple with "12 or 15 pieces of metal" that come off ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/burnout-5-damage-details)) | Arcade crumple with a clear "wrecked" state |
| Destruction AllStars | About 200 parts per vehicle; per-wheel health zones drive wheel loss ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=347606); [traxion.gg](https://traxion.gg/destruction-allstars-update-extends-season-one-and-improves-server-sync/)) | Handling penalties only in the last stretch of health |
| Destruction Derby (1995) | 10 HUD damage locations; front damage blows the radiator and kills the engine ([fan wiki](https://destructionderby.fandom.com/wiki/Destruction_Derby?oldid=7926)) | Genre template: spin points and engine death |

BeamNG models each vehicle as point masses ("nodes") joined by spring-damper "beams" that deform permanently and break. `breakGroup` makes a set of hinge beams fail together so a door comes off as one unit. `deformGroup` fires gameplay events such as broken glass or powertrain damage once a beam passes a strain threshold ([BeamNG docs](https://documentation.beamng.com/modding/vehicle/intro_jbeam/jbeamtips)). The 2,000 Hz step means **about 33 physics steps per rendered frame at 60 FPS**, which makes it CPU-bound ([BeamNG forum](https://www.beamng.com/threads/is-there-a-way-to-turn-up-physics.51381/)). Dated, unofficial forum posts describe roughly one vehicle per CPU thread ([BeamNG forum](https://beamng.com/threads/increasing-or-changing-physics-accuraccy-for-powerful-machines.2155)). A full-fidelity 20-car derby is therefore unrealistic. BeamNG still has an idea worth copying without the physics: separate a **coarse damage skeleton** from the render mesh, and use threshold triggers to switch gameplay states. The open-source Rigs of Rods (GPL-3.0, C++) is the reference implementation of the same concept ([GitHub](https://github.com/rigsofrods)).

### Tuning lessons: don't let one hit end the fun

The genre's clearest design lessons concern pacing. Wreckfest 2's early access, which launched 20 March 2025, chains damage: heavy frontal hits damage the radiator, which leaks coolant until the engine **overheats and blows a gasket** ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/racing/theres-barely-more-than-a-demo-in-wreckfest-2s-early-access-debut-but-i-cant-stop-playing-it)). Reviewers felt catastrophic damage came too easily, with one heavy hit able to end a race, and Bugbear later made cars last longer in Normal mode ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/racing/wreckfest-2s-latest-update-lets-you-drive-a-grotty-motorhome-thats-definitely-had-meth-cooked-in-it-and-ensures-that-realistic-damage-mode-is-now-more-realistic); [Xbox Tavern](https://www.xboxtavern.com/wreckfest-2-early-access-impressions-pc/)). Destruction AllStars' designers deliberately **ramped handling degradation only in the final stretch of health**, pulling cars to one side when a wheel is lost or damage is lopsided, so players would not abandon a car that drove badly ([Game Informer](https://www.gameinformer.com/2021/02/01/devs-share-a-behind-the-crash-look-at-destruction-allstars)). Wreckfest added damage indicators beside opponents' names so players could **pick targets they could beat** ([DSOGaming](https://www.dsogaming.com/news/next-car-game-new-update-adds-new-tracks-introduces-new-healthdamage-system/)). Burnout Paradise defines "wrecked" crisply: a lost wheel, too much engine damage, landing on the side or roof, or leaving the map ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnout_Paradise)). Destruction AllStars even raises adaptive-trigger resistance as damage grows ([GTPlanet](https://www.gtplanet.net/?p=109081)).

Licensing explains why mainstream racing games restrain damage. One developer told Jalopnik that two cars from two manufacturers in the same game **"have to have different damage levels"** ([Jalopnik](https://jalopnik.com/why-car-damage-sucks-in-racing-games-developer-explains-1850060288)). GRID's director said manufacturers ask "Is it realistic damage?" ([traxion.gg](https://traxion.gg/damage-inc-the-problem-with-crashes-in-racing-games)). The team's fictional-car plan avoids this entirely, as GTA does with its lookalike cars ([racinggames.gg](https://racinggames.gg/article/the-real-reason-racing-games-ditch-licensed-cars-for-fictional-brands)).

### The common stack: vertices, hinges, zones, masks, particles

Damage techniques recur across engines. **Vertex displacement at impact** pushes vertices within a radius of the contact point along the impact direction, with smooth falloff scaled by speed and a cap on depth; open community examples exist ([Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/now-with-source-mesh-deformation-on-impact/414788)). Doing the same in the vertex shader with distance falloff also works ([three-shader-deform](https://github.com/benschg/three-shader-deform)). **Lattice deformation** wraps the body in a control cage that absorbs each impact. The Unreal plugin Crushform deforms mesh, paint, glass and collision through a lattice, swings doors, hood and trunk loose before tearing them off, and advertises server-authoritative damage with client prediction ([Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/dmitriy-vergasov-crushform-vehicle-damage/2847033)). **Pre-baked or mesh-swap damage** shows up in products such as the Unreal "Easy Static Mesh Deformation Component" ([Fab](https://www.fab.com/listings/1c629d9e-044e-439b-873c-0b27f8e65902?lang=en)) and Unity's "Low Poly Destructible Cars" with swap-in damaged parts ([Unity Asset Store](https://assetstore.unity.com/packages/3d/vehicles/low-poly-destructible-cars-44832)). **Damage shading** stores a damage weight in vertex color and blends clean paint into scratches, chips and rust ([StraySpark](https://www.strayspark.studio/blog/blender-vertex-colors-game-engine-workflows)). The open-source softbodydriver goes further. Its shader dulls the clear coat, flakes paint to primer and exposes bare steel at the worst spots, and leaves paint intact on dents under about 3% crush ([GitHub](https://github.com/dibs999/softbodydriver/pull/8)).

Engine choice changes what can be bought off the shelf. **Unreal** has the most complete current options: Chaos Vehicles plus Fab plugins like Crushform. Crushform, however, depends on the Chaos Vehicles plugin, which is still marked Experimental ([Unreal forum](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/dmitriy-vergasov-crushform-vehicle-damage/2847033)). **Unity** has older but usable assets. Impact Deformable, for example, tracks structural damage numerically so a car can stop working at a set percentage, but its last version dates from 2019 ([TiplayStudio](https://tiplaystudio.gitbook.io/tiplay-assets/impact-deformable)). **Godot** and **three.js** have no turnkey car-damage solution. Godot users note that moving vertices is easy but making the result look right is the hard part ([Godot forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/how-would-i-make-a-car-in-godot-deform-similar-to-a-real-car/58203)). Versions and prices of these assets could not be verified because their pages were blocked.

### Multiplayer syncs damage events, not vertices

Full soft-body state is too large to stream. BeamNG community analysis points to "the huge amount of node information" as the obstacle and argues that remote cars need only be "close enough", not identical ([BeamNG forum](https://www.beamng.com/threads/multiplayer-theory.51413/)). The BeamMP multiplayer mod does sync vehicles and their damage between players ([EmeraldHost](https://docs.emeraldhost.de/en/gameserver/beammp)), but players report frame drops online and desynced cars, and the mod offers an experimental "simplified vehicles" option ([BeamMP forum](https://forum.beammp.com/t/bmp-online-experiencing-heavy-frame-drops-only-online/405654)). The design guidance that follows: the server owns zone health, mechanical states, panel states (latched, flapping, detached) and elimination, sent as small values when they change. Each significant hit is broadcast as a compact event (car, contact point, direction, magnitude, random seed), and every client runs the same deterministic deformation. Ranked by network cost, per-zone morph weights (a byte per zone) are cheapest, a lattice (tens to hundreds of quantized floats) comes next, and per-vertex or soft-body node state is worst.

## Real derbies run on a 60-second hit clock and a protected door

### The core rules are local but remarkably consistent

No national rulebook exists. Each fair or promoter writes its own, yet the core repeats across many of them. **The last car still moving and hitting wins.** Most rulebooks require an **aggressive hit every 60 seconds** ([Lebanon Area Fair 2026](https://cdn.saffire.com/files.ashx?t=fg&rid=LebanonAreaFair&f=2026_Demo_Derby26.pdf&cb=d7b97785); [Motorhead Events](https://motorheadevents.com/rules-registration/); [Montezuma County Fair](https://montezumacountyfair.com/demolition-derby-rules/)). Park County, Montana, allows two minutes to restart and strike after a stall ([Park County MT](https://www.parkcountymt.gov/uploads/files/pages/Demo-Derby-Rules-2018.pdf)). **Deliberate driver's-door hits disqualify** the attacker, with an exception for a car pushed into the door. Some tracks also warn that repeatedly using your own driver's door as a shield can bring disqualification ([Park County MT](https://www.parkcountymt.gov/uploads/files/pages/Demo-Derby-Rules-2018.pdf); [Shaner Promotions](https://shanerpromotions.com/demolition-derby-rules/)). **Sandbagging** (hiding to outlast others) is banned. At Motorhead Events, a stopped-for-sandbagging car gets **"a pink S sprayed on it"** ([Motorhead Events](https://motorheadevents.com/rules-registration/)). Eliminated drivers **break their stick or flag**. One county uses "Green-GO / Red-STOP / Black-Break your stick," and eliminated drivers stay in the car until the heat ends ([Park County 2016](http://www.parkcounty.org/dynamic/files/link_lists/Demo-Derby-Rules-2016_1469113691.pdf); [Nicollet County Fair](http://nicolletcountyfair.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/2021-Demolition-Derby-Rules-and-Regulations.pdf?6b0043=&6b0043=)). **One fire per heat is put out, and a second disqualifies** ([Wyoming State Fair](https://wystatefair.com/2022/05/06/demolition-derby-rules-2022/)). A car is also out if its driver's door opens, its gas tank ruptures or its driver's helmet comes off ([La Plata County Fair](https://laplatacountyfair.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/LA-PLATA-COUNTY-FAIR-DEMOLITION-DERBY.pdf)). Rollover rules vary. Alaska lets a rolled car continue if another car pushes it back onto its wheels, but no one may enter the arena to right it ([Alaska State Fair](https://www.alaskastatefair.org/site/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2025-Alaska-State-Fair-Demolition-Derby-Rules-Full-Size-Large-Cars.pdf)). Gaps remain: no source said whether officials use visible timers, or whether leaving the arena eliminates a car.

The driver's-door rule is the most interesting design hook. The door is a **forbidden target** for attackers, but it is also a **liability you cannot hide behind**, which creates real tension in positioning.

### Heats, consolations and features, across a ladder of classes

A fair derby usually runs several heats per class, a **consolation** round for non-qualifiers, and a **feature** final. At Carroll County, the consolation winner "receives $50 and advances to the Feature" ([Carroll County Fair](https://www.carrollcountyfair.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Demo-Derby-2025.pdf)). One Pennsylvania night listed "Junk Car Figure 8 Racing, Demo Derby Classes School Bus, Small Car, Super Stock, Youth Small Car, Chain N Go & Kids Powerwheels Class" ([ShowSlinger](https://app.showslinger.com/ticket_payment/18347/preview_ticket)). Promoters pair derbies with **school bus figure 8, enduros and trailer racing** ([Tour of Destruction](https://www.tourofdestruction.com/news)), and an Indiana fair runs a figure 8 with 10–12 retired school buses ([NWI Times](https://nwitimes.com/niche/bus-demolition-derby-brings-old-school-thrills-to-porter-county-fair-grandstand/article_59f298c5-4f10-5347-b608-c1e49c10d1a5.html)). Build classes form a natural progression ladder. **Stock** means "ABSOLUTELY NO WELDING ANYWHERE," and a non-stock car is moved to Modified ([Monroe County Fair](https://monroecountyfair.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/2024-Demolition-Derby-Rules-and-Entry-Form.pdf)). **Modified** permits specific bumper and rear-end-mount welds but no frame strengthening ([Bond County Fair](https://bondcofair.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2025-demolition-derby-rules-all-classes-2-14-1.pdf)). **Limited Weld** and **Full Build** go further ([Boulder County Fair](https://cdn.saffire.com/files.ashx?t=fg&rid=BoulderCountyFair&f=2024_Demo_Derby_Rules_Limited_Weld_MDGP.pdf); [Walla Walla Fair](https://cdn.saffire.com/files.ashx?t=fg&rid=WallaWallaFair&f=2026_WW_Fair_Demo_Derby_Full_Build_Rules.pdf&cb=b3e33571)). "Gut and go" concerns which drivetrain swaps are allowed rather than welding ([Dayton Fair](https://daytonfair.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Dayton-Fair-2022-Demo-Rules.pdf)). Compact classes are often defined by cylinders, front-wheel drive and wheelbase ([Performance Plus Tire](https://www.performanceplustire.com/blog/best-cars-for-demo-derby)). At the top tier, DEMO's first team **National Championship at Bristol Motor Speedway in October 2024 ran four days with a purse over $400,000** ([Bristol Motor Speedway](https://www.bristolmotorspeedway.com/media/news/worlds-most-prestigious-demolition-derby-coming-bms-october.html)). Heat sizes, how many cars advance from each heat, and team scoring could not be confirmed.

### Car prep: strip, relocate, cut, plate

Rulebooks consistently require removing all glass, lights and chrome. One says, "Do not break glass with hammer, remove it clean" ([Skyfire Productions](https://www.skyfireproductions.us/p/11/Preparation-of-Car); [Jackson County Jr Fair](https://www.jacksoncountyjrfair.org/derby-rules)). The **battery moves inside**, often to the passenger floor ([Antelope Valley Fair](https://avfair.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2026-Stock-DD-rules.pdf)). The **gas tank** is the most variable rule: replaced with a small metal or boat tank behind the seat, or strapped in its factory location ([Walla Walla Fair](https://cdn.saffire.com/files.ashx?t=fg&rid=WallaWallaFair&f=2026_WW_Fair_Demo_Derby_Full_Build_Rules.pdf&cb=b3e33571); [Alaska State Fair](https://www.alaskastatefair.org/site/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2025-Alaska-State-Fair-Demolition-Derby-Rules-Small-Compact-Cars.pdf)). Builders cut **hood holes, commonly about 12×12 inches**, for extinguisher access, and hoods are chained or bolted down with a capped number of fasteners ([Thomas Motorsport WV](https://thomasmotorsportwv.org/rules/full-size-class-8-cylinder-modified-demolition-derby/)). The **driver's door gets plate, angle iron or pipe and is padded inside**, and in one class it is "the ONLY door that may be welded" ([Calhoun County 2026](https://calhouncountyfairgrounds.com/wp-content/uploads/2026_Night-of-Destruction-Rules_4_17.pdf)). Front-wheel-drive compacts "sustain considerable back-end damage without being immobilized," so drivers run them in reverse ([Performance Plus Tire](https://www.performanceplustire.com/blog/best-cars-for-demo-derby)). Tire rules, cage rules and radiator-placement rules were not captured.

### Favored cars give a ready-made fictional roster

The legend is the **1964–66 Chrysler Imperial** (some sources say 1964–68). It kept a separate perimeter frame after the rest of Chrysler went unibody in 1960 and **is still banned from most derby events** ([Wikipedia: Chrysler D platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_D_platform_(RWD)); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_derby)). Today's everyman favorite is the **Ford Crown Victoria / Mercury Grand Marquis (Panther platform)**, especially ex-police cars, for its "incredibly strong full frame" ([HotCars](https://www.hotcars.com/crown-victoria-best-demolition-derby-cars/); [Performance Plus Tire](https://www.performanceplustire.com/blog/best-cars-for-demo-derby)). Chevy Impalas and Caprices are common picks, and **wagons** are favored for their "longer rear crush zone" ([SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1799514/demolition-derby-cars-typically-used)). In compact classes the GM W-body and Ford Escort are winning more events. The counterargument is that with front-wheel drive, "one solid hit to a front wheel can destroy the axle and immobilize you instantly" ([Autowise](https://autowise.com/?p=162403)). These map cleanly onto fictional archetypes, which is all the team needs: a banned, overbuilt 1960s land yacht; an ex-cop full-frame sedan as the balanced default; a box-body family sedan; a long-roof wagon built for reverse hits; a nimble but fragile FWD compact; and novelty minivans, pickups and buses. The "banned for being too strong" lore suits a tech-inspection gate or an "outlaw" exhibition mode.

### Safety, money and the Mad Dog

A **DOT-approved helmet**, eye protection, long pants and closed shoes are standard. Fire suits are often only recommended, but they are required for methanol cars ([Wyoming State Fair](https://wystatefair.com/2022/05/06/demolition-derby-rules-2022/); [La Plata County Fair](https://laplatacountyfair.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/LA-PLATA-COUNTY-FAIR-DEMOLITION-DERBY.pdf)). Purses at county fairs are small. A Vermont Field Days winner took **$3,500** in 2024 ([Addison Independent](https://www.addisonindependent.com/2024/08/15/tierney-prevails-in-demo-derby-to-take-home-3500-prize/)). Side awards include the **"Mad Dog"** for the hardest-hitting or craziest driver, worth $75 in Campbell County, Wyoming, $200 in Carver County, Minnesota, and $1,000 for large cars at the Alaska State Fair. Officials or the announcer choose the winner ([Gillette News Record](https://www.gillettenewsrecord.com/sports/local/article_243b42ba-053c-56d6-8755-93a1c3681811.html); [Carver County Fair](https://www.carvercountyfair.com/pdfs/2026-Demo-Derby-Rules-with-Entry-Form-FINAL.pdf); [Alaska State Fair](https://www.alaskastatefair.org/site/events/demolition-derby/)). Alaska also pays **$500 for Best Painted/Themed car** ([Alaska State Fair](https://www.alaskastatefair.org/site/events/demolition-derby/)). Together these suggest an economy that runs from a $75 county-fair side award to a six-figure national championship.

### Grandstand finales and a disputed origin

The popular origin story credits stock-car driver **Larry Mendelsohn at Islip Speedway, Long Island, in the late 1950s**. He reportedly noticed that a crowd cared more about his crash through the fence than about the race ([Sports Illustrated Vault](https://vault.si.com/vault/1975/10/20/cashing-in-on-crashing); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_derby)). A rival claim credits **Don Basile at Carrell Speedway, California, in 1947** ([Worcester Phoenix](https://worcesterphoenix.com/archive/features/00/07/21/NEWS_DEMO_DERBY.html)). ABC's *Wide World of Sports* televised the World Championship Demolition Derby from the mid-1960s until 1992 ([Islip Speedway, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islip_Speedway)). Today the derby is often a fair's grandstand **"grand finale,"** where hometown drivers matter to the crowd ([Mason County Press](https://www.masoncountypress.com/2025/08/10/demo-derby-is-grand-finale-to-86th-west-michigan-fair/)). Several events claim "world's largest" titles: the indoor Blizzard Bash in Kansas draws 250+ drivers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blizzard_Bash)), and the Canfield Fair in Ohio bills a "World's Largest Demolition Derby" ([Canfield Fair](https://www.canfieldfair.com/events/2026/worlds-largest-demolition-derby)). Bristol markets DEMO as "the Last Great Colosseum" ([BMS](https://www.bristolmotorspeedway.com/media/news/demo-returns-last-great-colosseum-oct.html)). Abroad, UK **banger racing** has three rule presets, only one of them full-contact ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banger_racing)), and Australia's first derby, in 1963 in Adelaide, drew a reported 20,000 people ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_derby)). That suggests a career arc: county fair, then state fair, then indoor winter bash, then stadium "colosseum." Arena construction (mud, barriers, berms) and atmosphere details could not be sourced.

## Rattle-can folk art inside mandatory 18-inch numbers

### Loud, cheap, hand-done, and often a family project

Derby cars "are repainted, often in loud, garish designs and on low budgets," and spray paint marks names, slogans and IDs ([Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demo_derby)). One observer said the spray-painted numbers "look like graffiti" ([Patterns and Tones](https://patternsandtones.blogspot.com/2013/07/demolition-derby-rock-and-roll.html)). The themes are folk and pop culture. At Maine's Union Fair, cars included a rusted car sprayed "GIT ER DONE", a station wagon painted as a school bus, and a pink Mary Kay-colored car chosen to stand out ([Sports Illustrated](https://www.si.com/more-sports/2018/09/26/demolition-derby-union-fair-maine)). At Marshfield, Massachusetts, a family painted their minivan with a man riding a shark on one side and SpongeBob on the other ([Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2025/08/22/metro/demolition-derby-marshfield-fair/)). A 5th-grade class painted their art teacher's Canfield car with a student's "Venom and Carnage" design ([WYTV](https://www.wytv.com/news/local-news/5th-grade-class-paints-car-for-canfield-fair-demolition-derby/)). A teen's family-painted "Mean Girls" car won Best in Show in Wyoming ([Wyoming News](https://www.wyomingnews.com/news/local_news/teen-s-grit-proves-demolition-derbies-have-no-limits/article_449cac3a-fc96-4f08-af35-0071c972dd47.html)). Iowa teens painted five cars on social issues, and "Climate Change" won People's Choice ([University of Iowa](https://education.uiowa.edu/news/2025/07/just-crushing-demolition-derby)). In Pennsylvania, high school band students paint buses, including a Star Wars montage, for a bus derby's best-paint award ([Observer-Reporter](https://cs.observer-reporter.com/?p=80089)). Sponsorship is grassroots. An Ontario driver offered to paint local business names on his car ([EloraFergusToday](https://www.elorafergustoday.com/local-news/cheaper-than-therapy-demolition-derby-draws-driver-back-to-erin-12851886)), and auto-body chain Maaco has backed a "Best Appearance" trophy ([Big Heavy World](https://bigheavyworld.com/blog-archive/blog/2016/09/04/big-heavy-takes-demolition-derby-prize-ian-mccullough-captains-best-appearing-car)). No citable sources were found for how common flames, patriotic, memorial or pun-name themes are, or for typical sponsor layouts. Those are anecdotal.

### Rulebooks fix the frame the art lives in

Rulebooks set the hard constraints. Numbers must be **at least 18 inches tall in contrasting paint on each front door and the roof**, with the driver's door painted white at Miles City ([Miles City rules](https://milescitychamber.com/mcLuci/uploads/2017/06/2017-Derby-Rules.pdf); [Moses Lake Roundup](https://www.rodeoticket.com/rodeos/moses-lake-roundup/2024/register)). The mandated driver's-door color varies by event: **red** in Alaska's full-size class ([Alaska State Fair](https://www.alaskastatefair.org/site/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026-Alaska-State-Fair-Demolition-Derby-Rules-Full-Size-Large-Cars.pdf)), **yellow** at the Cumberland Fair ([Cumberland Fair](https://cumberlandfair.com/wp-content/uploads/2022-derby-rules-02.pdf)), or simply contrasting ([Carlton County Fair](https://carltoncountyfairmn.com/demolition-derby-rules)). Pine County requires a **roof sign of at least 12×12 inches** and bans offensive words or pictures anywhere on the car ([Pine County Fair](https://www.pinecountyfair.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/DerbyRulesPacket.pdf)). In Australia, Maitland officials may remove signage they judge in bad taste ([Maitland Showground](https://maitlandshowground.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Maitland-Derby-Rules.pdf)). The bright paint and contrasting driver's door also help other drivers avoid the forbidden door ([Advance Auto Parts](https://content.advanceautoparts.com/car-culture/community/demolition-derbies-for-the-thrill-and-the-skill-of-it)). Rattle cans are the confirmed default tool. Detailed technique sources (house paint with rollers, stencils, masking, primer) could not be loaded.

### Games split between vector layer editors and painted texture skins

Racing games use two main customization models. **Vector layer editors** (Forza, Gran Turismo, Need for Speed) stack shapes and decals per car zone. Forza Horizon 3 allowed up to 3,000 layers on each side and 1,000 on each bumper ([Forza forums](https://forums.forza.net/t/forza-horizon-3-game-guide-faq/36955/51)). GT7 accepts custom decals as **SVG files of at most 15 KB** uploaded through a web tool ([RacingGames.gg](https://racinggames.gg/article/how-to-upload-decals-to-gran-turismo-7)). **Texture skins** are painted against a UV template outside the game. BeamNG provides a non-mirrored UV map for paint schemes, and because damaged material variants take priority, modders must reskin those too ([BeamNG docs](https://documentation.beamng.com/modding/materials/vehicle/skinsystem/)). Rocket League offers preset decals and colors officially, with custom images only through PC mods ([Kaizen RL](https://kaizenrl.gumroad.com/l/custom-paint)). The closest genre precedent is **Wreckfest 2's "CRAP-IT" tool (November 2025)**, which treats rust, dents, paint removal, dirt, words and icons as cosmetic choices that everyone sees in multiplayer ([OnlineRaceDriver](https://www.onlineracedriver.com/2025/11/04/wreckfest-2-content-update-4-adds-visual-customisation/); [Traxion](https://traxion.gg/wreckfest-2s-latest-update-adds-crap-it-customisation-tool/)). Spray-can mechanics exist mostly in sims: My Summer Car's cans paint whole parts and leave dull marks over rust ([My Summer Car Wiki](https://my-summer-car.fandom.com/wiki/Spray_can)). Bomb Rush Cyberfunk sidesteps freehand drawing by stamping graffiti from **stick-direction sequences** ([BRC Wiki](https://bombrushcyberfunk.wiki.gg/wiki/Graffiti)). That is a useful controller-friendly model.

### Paint must live in UV space so it survives the crumple

The key technical constraint for a derby game is that paint has to deform with the car. Mesh-baked and projected decal systems "will not work nicely with skinned mesh renderers or other meshes that get distorted at runtime" ([Driven Decals](https://github.com/Anatta336/driven-decals/blob/master/Documentation~/DrivenDecals.md)). Runtime mask painting requires painted objects to share a UV space ([Unity discussion](https://discussions.unity.com/t/shader-that-erases-part-of-a-texture-to-show-another-texture-based-on-a-decal/463832)). Paint should therefore be stored in a per-car **UV-space texture**, ideally baked from a compact layer list, and a separate damage mask should reveal the real paint stack beneath it: **primer shows grey or white, bare metal bright silver, plastic bumpers black** ([Wikipedia: Automotive paint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_paint)). Moderation needs planning from day one. Forza routes reports to a human queue where report volume does not change priority ([Forza Support](https://support.forza.net/hc/en-us/articles/4409070620819-How-to-Block-Mute-or-Report-a-Player-or-Report-Inappropriate-UGC-in-Forza-Horizon)). Forza Horizon 6's livery-moderation backlash in October–November 2026 forced Playground to clarify that itasha (anime-themed) liveries are allowed but sexually suggestive content is not. Its reported figures were about **21,900 reports, of which 3% led to penalties**; these are company-reported numbers relayed by the press ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/forza-horizon-6-livery-moderation-major-community-backlash/)). The real derby rule against offensive words and pictures gives an in-world framing for the same policy.

## Design takeaways for a fictional-car derby

The table turns the evidence into concrete decisions. These are the synthesis's recommendations, not reported facts; each row notes the evidence that grounds it.

| Area | Recommendation | Grounding |
|---|---|---|
| Damage architecture | Layer five systems: a deformable body shell, hinged-then-detachable panels (hood, trunk, doors, bumpers), 6–10 health zones (front, rear, left, right, four wheels, engine, roof), a shader wear mask, and particles | The shared recipe across Wreckfest, FlatOut, Destruction AllStars and the Unreal plugins |
| Deformation | Use a coarse lattice or per-zone morph targets rather than soft-body; compute dent depth from closing speed minus an elastic threshold, scaled by zone stiffness | Campbell's linear crush model; BeamNG's 2,000 Hz cost |
| What kills a car | Model radiator, steering and tie rods, each wheel, driveshaft and engine as systems; eliminate on an engine timeout, a lost drive, two or more wheels gone, or immobility | Derby practice: radiator most vulnerable, driveshaft-out "classic" stop |
| Pacing | Keep per-hit damage modest; ramp handling penalties only in the last ~30% of zone health; add a "sputtering" last-chance window | About 52 hits per event; Wreckfest 2's one-hit complaints; Destruction AllStars' late ramp |
| Tactics | Make the rear a big, low-criticality buffer and the front the lethal target; reversing should be strong but harder to steer | 55% rear-end hits; "hit from the back" folk wisdom |
| Car archetypes | Body-on-frame cars take panel damage but treat the frame as a separate pool whose damage causes crabbing or pulling; unibody compacts have single-use crush zones and fragile front axles | Frame-damage taxonomy; FWD compact reports |
| Rules | Last car moving wins; 60-second hit timer with an on-screen countdown; penalty for driver's-door hits and for door-shielding; two-strike fires; anti-sandbagging "pink S" shame mark; "break the stick" as forfeit | Recurring fair rulebook rules |
| Structure | Fair night = heats, then consolation, then feature; class ladder Stock → Limited Weld → Modified → Full Build; novelty modes: school bus figure 8, minivans, trailer racing | Fair formats and class definitions |
| Scoring and economy | Placement plus a "Mad Dog" aggression meter, a Best Appearing vote, and spin-style hit points; purses from about $75 side awards to six-figure team championships | Fair awards; Destruction Derby's 2/4/10 spin points ([fan page](https://www.staff.ncl.ac.uk/d.g.alsop/dd.htm)) |
| Paint tools | A small vector layer editor (shapes, fonts, number templates, sponsor stickers), a freehand rattle-can brush with overspray and drips, and a CRAP-IT-style rust and dirt layer; a gesture-stamp option for controller players | Forza/GT editors; CRAP-IT; Bomb Rush Cyberfunk |
| Paint rules | Fixed slots for 18-inch-equivalent numbers on both doors and roof, an event-mandated driver's-door color, an optional roof-sign mesh, a contrast check, and a text filter | Miles City, Alaska and Pine County rulebooks |
| Paint and damage | Store liveries as a layer list baked to a UV-space texture; a damage mask exposes clear coat, then primer, then bare metal, then rust, with mud accumulating; offer a post-match before/after photo | Decal limits on deforming meshes; softbodydriver shader |
| Multiplayer | Server-authoritative zone and mechanical state; broadcast hit events with seeds for deterministic client-side deformation; cosmetic debris local to each client | BeamMP struggles; Crushform's model |
| Sharing and safety | Vector-only or curated stickers, no raw image upload at launch; report-to-human queue; option to show only friends' liveries; publish clear rules | GT7 SVG limits; FH6 backlash |

Two constraints specific to this project need early attention. First, **the engine is not yet chosen**, so the damage design should be engine-agnostic: zone health, mechanical states and hit events are plain data, and the deformation layer can be swapped in. Unreal offers the most off-the-shelf help (Chaos Vehicles plus a Fab damage plugin, with the caveat that Chaos Vehicles is still marked Experimental). Unity has older assets that would need custom work. Godot and web stacks require building deformation in-house, where per-zone morph targets are the safest first step. Second, the **CC0 low-poly starting models** will likely need preparation (inference, not verified against any specific asset pack). Few vertices means per-vertex dents look faceted. That can read as an intentional stylized look, or it can be fixed by subdividing the deformable regions or using artist-authored morph targets per zone. Free models often ship as a single mesh with mirrored or overlapping UVs. Paint needs a clean, non-overlapping UV layout (as BeamNG's paint UV map illustrates), and hinged panels must be split into separate objects with pivots at the hinge line. Fictional cars let the team push damage as far as fun allows; the remaining limit is the team's own tuning.

## Conclusion

The research changes the central design question from "how realistically can we crumple a car?" to "which systems fail, and in what order?" Real derbies are wars of attrition decided by a handful of vulnerable parts: the radiator, the tie rods, the wheels and the driveshaft. That is good news for a small team. Convincing derby damage needs a believable *story of decline* (steam, a pull to one side, a dragging wheel, an engine revving with no movement) far more than it needs expensive soft-body physics, and the systems that tell that story are cheap to network. The real rulebook also solves design problems that would otherwise need inventing: the 60-second hit clock stops camping, the protected driver's door creates a forbidden weak spot, and two-strike fires add drama.

The paint side holds the genre's most distinctive opportunity. In racing games liveries are protected showpieces; in a derby game they are **meant to be destroyed**, and real derby culture is built around families, kids and classrooms decorating a car for one glorious wreck. A game that makes painting quick, communal and expressive, and then shows that paint scraped back to primer and bare steel by the feature, captures something no licensed racer can. The biggest open risks are tuning (Wreckfest 2 shows how easily one-hit eliminations frustrate players) and community moderation of shared liveries (Forza Horizon 6 shows how quickly unclear rules turn into backlash). Both should be planned in from the first prototype. Several factual gaps remain because pages were blocked: crush coefficients for classic sedans, heat sizes and team formats, arena construction, and painting technique guides. These are worth revisiting with full-page access before production commits to numbers.
