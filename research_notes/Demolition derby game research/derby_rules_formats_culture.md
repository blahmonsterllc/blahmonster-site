# Real-World Demolition Derby: Rules, Formats, Car Prep, Favored Cars, Culture

> Method note: In this session, WebFetch could not reach any outside host (Wikipedia, fair-rulebook PDF hosts, countyfairsusa.net and others all failed DNS resolution). Every finding below therefore comes from **web-search result snippets** of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Quoted rule text is what the search engine returned from those documents. The core rules (60-second hit timer, no driver-door hits, sandbagging bans, battery moved inside, hood holes) appear consistently across many independent fair rulebooks, so confidence in them is high. ## 1. Typical rules: hit timers, driver-door rule, eliminations, how a winner is decided

### Takeaway
No national standard exists: each fair or promoter writes its own rulebook. The common core is consistent, though: the last car still moving and making hits wins. Each car must make an aggressive hit about every 60 seconds (range 60 seconds to 2 minutes). Deliberate driver-door hits and "sandbagging" (hiding to outlast others) get a car disqualified. A car is out if it stalls past the timer, its driver breaks their stick or flag, it has a second fire, or its driver's door opens or the driver's helmet comes off.

### Cited Findings
- **Hit timer.** Many rulebooks require an aggressive hit every 60 seconds:
  - The Lebanon Area Fair (PA) 2026 rules use 60 seconds. — [Lebanon Area Fair 2026](https://cdn.saffire.com/files.ashx?t=fg&rid=LebanonAreaFair&f=2026_Demo_Derby26.pdf&cb=d7b97785)
  - At Motorhead Events, a car is out after 1 minute without an aggressive hit. — [Motorhead Events](https://motorheadevents.com/rules-registration/)
  - Another rule set allows "60 seconds to make contact with another active car" and disqualifies stalled or non-moving cars. — [Montezuma County Fair](https://montezumacountyfair.com/demolition-derby-rules/)
- **Longer timers exist.** A derby FAQ gives the window as "every 60-90 seconds." — [go-derby.com FAQ](https://go-derby.com/faq). The search summary flagged other go-derby.com pages as unreliable, so this is weak support.
- **Restart window.** Park County (MT) allows "2 minutes to get started and strike another vehicle" after a stall. — [Park County MT 2018](https://www.parkcountymt.gov/uploads/files/pages/Demo-Derby-Rules-2018.pdf)
- **Driver's door.** Deliberate hits are disqualified at Lebanon. — [Lebanon Area Fair 2023](https://cdn.saffire.com/files.ashx?t=fg&rid=LebanonAreaFair&f=2023_demo_rules(1).pdf)
  - Park County disqualifies a car for hitting a driver's door, except when that car was pushed into it. — [Park County MT 2018](https://www.parkcountymt.gov/uploads/files/pages/Demo-Derby-Rules-2018.pdf)
  - Some tracks penalize an accidental or door-defense hit only if officials judge it careless or intentional. They also warn that repeatedly using your own driver's door as a shield can bring disqualification. — [Shaner Promotions (Central IL)](https://shanerpromotions.com/demolition-derby-rules/); [MAP Motorsports](https://mapmotorsports.com/rules-2)
- **Sandbagging** is banned almost everywhere. — [Lebanon 2026](https://cdn.saffire.com/files.ashx?t=fg&rid=LebanonAreaFair&f=2026_Demo_Derby26.pdf&cb=d7b97785); [Park County MT](https://www.parkcountymt.gov/uploads/files/pages/Demo-Derby-Rules-2018.pdf)
  - At Motorhead Events, if the derby is stopped for sandbagging, the offending car gets "a pink S sprayed on it." — [Motorhead Events](https://motorheadevents.com/rules-registration/)
  - At Park County, officials stop the derby and mark the car. — [Park County MT](https://www.parkcountymt.gov/uploads/files/pages/Demo-Derby-Rules-2018.pdf)
- **Stick or flag elimination.** Breaking the stick usually means you are out.
  - One county's flag system is "Green-GO / Red-STOP / Black-Break your stick." — [Park County 2016](http://www.parkcounty.org/dynamic/files/link_lists/Demo-Derby-Rules-2016_1469113691.pdf)
  - Another rulebook says an eliminated driver must "shut off the engine, break the stick, and stay in the car until the heat is over." — [Nicollet County Fair 2021](http://nicolletcountyfair.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/2021-Demolition-Derby-Rules-and-Regulations.pdf?6b0043=&6b0043=); [Herkimer County Fair](https://www.herkimercountyfair.org/demolition-derby)
- **Fire.** A common rule: "one fire per heat will be put out, and a second fire per heat will cause disqualification." — [Wyoming State Fair 2022](https://wystatefair.com/2022/05/06/demolition-derby-rules-2022/); [Deep South Fair](https://deepsouthfairgrounds.com/derby-rules/)
- **Other automatic disqualifications:** "if the driver door opens, gas tank ruptures, or two fires occur, or if a helmet comes off during heats." — [La Plata County Fair](https://laplatacountyfair.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/LA-PLATA-COUNTY-FAIR-DEMOLITION-DERBY.pdf)
- **Rollovers.** Rules vary:
  - Some say any fire or rollover may cause disqualification.
  - Another allows a rolled car to keep going if another car pushes it back onto its wheels; no one may enter the arena to right it. — [Alaska State Fair 2025 full-size](https://www.alaskastatefair.org/site/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2025-Alaska-State-Fair-Demolition-Derby-Rules-Full-Size-Large-Cars.pdf); [Park County 2016](http://www.parkcounty.org/dynamic/files/link_lists/Demo-Derby-Rules-2016_1469113691.pdf)

### Inferences
- A faithful core loop: last car moving wins. A per-car hit timer (default 60 seconds, with an on-screen countdown) prevents camping. Players are penalized for hitting a driver's door, which is an explicit "weak spot you are forbidden to target." Fires work as a two-strike system.
- The anti-sandbagging rule and the "pink S" mark are good flavor for an AI-shaming or player-penalty system.
- Real rules make the driver's door both protected (you can't hit it) and a liability (you can't use it to shield yourself). That gives a natural risk/reward design hook.
- "Breaking the stick" is an iconic, voluntary way to forfeit. In a game it could be a "tap out" button, or the visual cue when a car is eliminated.

### Gaps
- I couldn't read full rulebooks, so I have no exact counts of how many fair rulebooks use 60 versus 90 versus 120 seconds.
- No sources on whether officials use visible timers or judge by eye. (They likely judge by eye, but that is not sourced.)
- Not confirmed: whether a car that leaves the arena boundary is eliminated in general. No snippet addressed "rolling out of the arena."

## 2. Event formats and classes

### Takeaway
A typical fair derby splits each class into several **heats**. The top finishers from each heat (plus winners of a **consolation** round for non-qualifiers) advance to a **feature**, the main event. Events run many classes:
- full-size (V8 "big car")
- compact or small car, often 4- or 6-cylinder front-wheel drive
- minivan
- pickup truck
- school bus
- kids' Power Wheels
- "chain-and-go," limited weld, and full build

Racing hybrids such as figure-8 racing, school bus figure 8, trailer racing and enduros are often run on the same night. Team derbies are a growing top-tier format, for example DEMO's $400k+ team championship at Bristol.

  - Preble County has "no pay outs or trophies for consolation heat." Its consolation round is simply a chance to reach the feature. — [Preble County Fair 2026](https://www.preblecountyfair.com/__static/jdj5jdewjgdpymyvm3g1v2xsytq5uddb/DEMOLITION-DERBY.pdf)

  - A later D.E.M.O.3 at Bristol expected 250+ competition entries. — [BMS DEMO returns](https://www.bristolmotorspeedway.com/media/news/demo-returns-last-great-colosseum-oct.html); [BMS DEMO3](https://www.bristolmotorspeedway.com/media/news/combat-motorsports-rumbles-into-bristol-this-weekend-with-demo3.html)

### Inferences
- Natural game structure: a career or fair night = heats → consolation ("last chance") → feature. Add side awards (see section 5) and novelty classes (buses, minivans, Power Wheels, trailer figure 8) as unlockable modes.
- Build tiers map neatly onto progression and balancing classes: Stock (no welds) → Limited Weld → Modified → Full Build. "Chain-and-go" is another tier: doors and panels are held with chain or wire instead of welds.
- Three race hybrids could each be a separate mode:
  - **Figure-8:** racing with a crossover intersection where T-bones happen.
  - **Enduro:** long-distance racing where contact is allowed.
  - **Trailer racing:** cars tow trailers that get destroyed.

## 3. Car prep

### Takeaway
Before a car may compete, builders typically remove:
- all glass, lights, chrome and plastic
- loose debris
- interior flammables

They also:
- move the battery inside the car, usually to the passenger floorboard, and secure it
- deal with the gas tank: replace it with a metal or boat tank secured inside behind the seat, or strap it in place (varies by event)
- cut hood holes (commonly about 12"×12") for fire-extinguisher access
- reinforce and pad the driver's door, and paint it with the car number

How much welding is allowed (doors, frame, bumpers) defines the class.

### Cited Findings
- **Glass.** All glass, lights and chrome must be removed; "loose glass found and you will not be allowed to run"; "Do not break glass with hammer, remove it clean." — [Jackson County Junior Fair](https://www.jacksoncountyjrfair.org/derby-rules); [Skyfire Productions](https://www.skyfireproductions.us/p/11/Preparation-of-Car)
- **Battery.** "battery must be moved inside the car and securely fastened"; "You may only use 1 car type battery." Some rules specify the front passenger floorboard. — [Antelope Valley Fair 2026 Stock](https://avfair.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2026-Stock-DD-rules.pdf); [Bond County Fair 2025](https://bondcofair.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2025-demolition-derby-rules-all-classes-2-14-1.pdf)
- **Gas tank.** This rule varies the most:
  - Some require the stock tank replaced with a metal or approved boat tank, and say "you cannot move the OEM tank to the inside of the car."
  - Others allow relocating the tank to the rear-seat area or behind the driver's seat.
  - Some say "Gas tank must be secured with a strap in factory location."
  - Sources: [Walla Walla Fair 2026 Full Build](https://cdn.saffire.com/files.ashx?t=fg&rid=WallaWallaFair&f=2026_WW_Fair_Demo_Derby_Full_Build_Rules.pdf&cb=b3e33571); [Hanover Ag compact](https://www.hanoverag.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/HanoverAg-CompactCar-DemoDerby.pdf); [Selkirk Fair compact](https://selkirkfairandrodeo.jigsy.com/files/documents/08e5e2bc-6c23-4428-8775-cc430fd073b8.pdf)
- **Hood holes.** Common specifications:
  - "cut a 12×12 inch hole in the center of the hood"
  - "two holes up to 12" x 12" on both sides of the carburetor for fire purposes or one 6" diameter hole"
  - openings "cut 15 inches in the top of the hood"

  Hood fasteners are usually capped at a set number of bolts or chains, and the hood often must stay on its hinges. — [Thomas Motorsport WV](https://thomasmotorsportwv.org/rules/full-size-class-8-cylinder-modified-demolition-derby/); [MAP Motorsports](https://mapmotorsports.com/rules-2); [Skyfire Productions](https://www.skyfireproductions.us/p/11/Preparation-of-Car)
- **Driver's door protection** (Calhoun County MI, 2026): "Driver's door must have some kind of driver's protection which will consist of plate, angle iron or pipe. Must be bolted, welded or 9 gauge wired"; "Inside driver's door must be padded"; "Driver's door is the ONLY door that may be welded" (in that class). The small car class requires the driver's door painted white with a large readable number. — [Calhoun County 2026 Night of Destruction](https://calhouncountyfairgrounds.com/wp-content/uploads/2026_Night-of-Destruction-Rules_4_17.pdf)
  - Alaska State Fair 2026 full-size: doors bolted shut; "The Driver's door may have window netting for driver protection." — [Alaska 2026 Full Size](https://www.alaskastatefair.org/site/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026-Alaska-State-Fair-Demolition-Derby-Rules-Full-Size-Large-Cars.pdf)
  - Yorkton Exhibition (SK, Canada) 2025: "Driver's door must be reinforced with channel iron, or box steel." — [Yorkton 2025](https://yorktonexhibition.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2025-Derby-Rules.pdf)
- **Welding by class.** Stock means no welding at all. Modified allows specific bumper and rear-end-mount welds but no frame strengthening. Limited Weld and Full Build go progressively further. (See section 2 for citations.)
- **FWD builder tips:**
  - Front-wheel-drive cars can "sustain considerable back-end damage without being immobilized," so drivers often run them in reverse, hitting with the rear and protecting the engine and drive wheels.
  - For FWD builds, "strip extra weight aggressively and reinforce trunk seams."
  - Sources: [Performance Plus Tire](https://www.performanceplustire.com/blog/best-cars-for-demo-derby); [Autowise](https://autowise.com/?p=162403)

### Inferences
- **Garage and upgrade system, using real-world prep items:**
  - strip glass and interior (weight and safety)
  - battery and gas tank relocation (reduces fire risk)
  - hood holes
  - driver's door plate
  - bumper swap or weld
  - chain or wire versus welded doors
  - frame welding, as a tier unlocked by class
- **Visual identity:** windowless shells, a giant spray-painted number on the white driver's door, hood holes, and chained or wired hoods.
- **Driving style:** "back up to hit" (protecting the radiator and front wheels) is authentic and should be the default AI behavior, especially for FWD compacts.

- Couldn't confirm whether roll cages are allowed (and what kind) in each class. Cages and bars are typically limited to a "driver's cage" or "dash bar," but this is not sourced here.
- Radiator rules (stock location versus relocated) were not captured.

## 4. Favored cars and why

### Takeaway
The legendary pick is the 1964–66 (some say 1964–68) **Chrysler Imperial**: it kept a separate full perimeter frame after the rest of Chrysler's lineup moved to unibody, which made it so tough it is banned from most derbies. Today's common full-size picks are body-on-frame rear-wheel-drive V8s, above all the Ford Crown Victoria and Mercury Grand Marquis (Panther platform, including police interceptors), plus Chevy Impala and Caprice. Station wagons are favored for their long rear crush zone. In compact classes, the GM W-body and the Ford Escort are the most-cited FWD picks.

### Cited Findings
- **Imperial ban.**
  - Imperials kept separate full perimeter frames through the 1966 model year, while the rest of Chrysler went unibody in 1960. Demolition derbies "have since outlawed the cars from most competitions." — [Chrysler D platform (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysler_D_platform_(RWD)); [Demolition derby (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_derby)
  - The car achieved "near-legendary status for its crashworthiness, and it is still banned from most derby events." Wikipedia cites 1964–66; other sources say 1964–68. — [Demolition derby (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_derby); [MotorBiscuit](https://www.motorbiscuit.com/best-car-demolition-derby/)
  - Fans describe a "frame so strong, and the front end so over-engineered" that organizers banned it because "it wasn't fair to the other drivers." This is enthusiast opinion. — [Allpar forum](https://www.allpar.com/threads/why-is-chrysler-so-good-in-demolition-derby.168489/); [Nick's Demolition Derby Page](https://www.oocities.org/dudesteer1/secrets.htm)
  - Banned-car discussion: [SlashGear: banned cars](https://www.slashgear.com/1802267/demolition-derby-cars-banned-from-events-explainedl); [Henry's Daily Factoids: "The Unwelcome Imperial"](https://henrysdailyfactoids.blogspot.com/2016/05/the-unwelcome-imperial.html); [Curbside Classic](https://www.curbsideclassic.com/cc-outtake-painkiller/)
- **Crown Victoria.** "Affordable, everywhere, and famously tough"; police-interceptor versions are especially rugged. Its "incredibly strong full frame" and "durable 4.6L V8" are the usual reasons given. — [Performance Plus Tire](https://www.performanceplustire.com/blog/best-cars-for-demo-derby); [HotCars](https://www.hotcars.com/crown-victoria-best-demolition-derby-cars/)
- **Other popular full-size cars:** Chevy Impala and Caprice, GM W-body, and Crown Vic and Grand Marquis wagons. Wagons are recommended for their "longer rear crush zone." — [Performance Plus Tire](https://performanceplustire.com/Blog/best-cars-for-demo-derby); [SlashGear: typical cars](https://www.slashgear.com/1799514/demolition-derby-cars-typically-used)
- **Compacts.**
  - FWD cars like the GM W-body and Ford Escort are "winning more events every year, especially in compact classes." — [Performance Plus Tire](https://www.performanceplustire.com/blog/best-cars-for-demo-derby)
  - Counterpoint opinion: with FWD, "one solid hit to a front wheel can destroy the axle and immobilize you instantly." — [Autowise](https://autowise.com/?p=162403)
- **Australia:** most derby cars are older Australian-made sedans and wagons. — [Demolition derby (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_derby)

### Inferences
- **Fictionalized roster archetypes** (a game would need non-licensed names):
  - **"The Imperial"-type land yacht:** an overpowered, separate-frame 1960s luxury barge that is banned from normal classes. It could be an unlockable "outlaw" car or a "banned car" exhibition mode.
  - **Panther-style full-size sedan or ex-cop car:** the balanced, everyman default.
  - **GM B-body type sedan:** an Impala- or Caprice-style car.
  - **Long-roof wagon:** a strong rear crush zone, good for reverse hitting.
  - **FWD compact:** light and nimble, best at reverse hits, with a fragile front axle.
  - **Specialty vehicles:** minivan, pickup, school bus.
- The "banned for being too strong" lore is excellent flavor for in-game tech inspection or a rulebook system.

## 5. Safety rules, prize money, scoring and judging

### Takeaway
A DOT-approved helmet is near-universal, along with eye protection and long pants or a firesuit. Firesuits are often only recommended, except for cars running methanol fuel. Fire crews put out a first fire, and a second fire disqualifies the car. County-fair purses are small: winning a class feature is typically worth hundreds to a few thousand dollars, and side awards run $75–$1,000. Side awards include "Mad Dog" (the hardest-hitting or craziest driver, chosen by officials or the announcer), best-appearing or best-painted car, and heat-win money. Big national events pay much more, e.g. DEMO at Bristol with a $400k+ total purse.

### Cited Findings
- **Safety gear.** "DOT approved helmet, safety glasses, long pants or fire suit, closed toe shoes or boots are required."
  - Another rulebook says "fire suit is recommended."
  - A third requires full fire suits for any car running methanol fuel.
  - Helmet and belt must stay on during the heat.
  - Sources: [Wyoming State Fair 2022](https://wystatefair.com/2022/05/06/demolition-derby-rules-2022/); [Deep South Fair](https://deepsouthfairgrounds.com/derby-rules/); [La Plata County Fair](https://laplatacountyfair.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/LA-PLATA-COUNTY-FAIR-DEMOLITION-DERBY.pdf)
- **Fire handling:** the first fire per heat is extinguished; a second disqualifies the car. — [Wyoming State Fair 2022](https://wystatefair.com/2022/05/06/demolition-derby-rules-2022/)
- **Mad Dog award.**
  - Alaska State Fair: $1,000 for large cars. — [Alaska State Fair Demolition Derby page](https://www.alaskastatefair.org/site/events/demolition-derby/)
  - Carver County (MN): a $200 check in each class to the driver with "the most hits or craziest driving," judged by officials. — [Carver County Fair 2026](https://www.carvercountyfair.com/pdfs/2026-Demo-Derby-Rules-with-Entry-Form-FINAL.pdf)

## 6. Culture, history and atmosphere

### Takeaway
The sport's popular origin story credits stock-car driver **Larry Mendelsohn** at **Islip Speedway, Long Island**, in the late 1950s (often given as 1958). That account is disputed: one claim credits **Don Basile** at Carrell Speedway (Gardena, California) in 1947. Derbies boomed through 1960s county fairs. ABC's *Wide World of Sports* televised the World Championship Demolition Derby from the mid-1960s to 1992. The derby remains a grandstand finale at Midwest and rural county and state fairs. "World's largest" titles are mostly self-promotion: Canfield Fair (OH), the Blizzard Bash (KS, indoor, 250+ drivers). The UK's version is banger racing; Australia's first derby drew a reported 20,000 people in 1963.

### Cited Findings
- **Mendelsohn origin story.** According to the popular version, stock car driver Larry Mendelsohn organized and promoted the first true demolition derby on Long Island, New York, in the late 1950s. — [Demolition derby (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_derby)
  - Story details: Mendelsohn was "a former dishwasher, banana boat loader, gas station operator and coal shoveler." He crashed his stock car through the fence into the grandstands during a race and noticed the crowd was more fascinated by the wreck than by the race. — [Sports Illustrated Vault, "Cashing In On Crashing" (1975)](https://vault.si.com/vault/1975/10/20/cashing-in-on-crashing); [POV "Speedo" History of the Demo Derby](https://archive.pov.org/speedo/history/)
  - Islip Speedway: a 0.2-mile oval in Islip, NY, open 1947–1984. The derby idea is described as having been patented by Mendelsohn, who worked there. — [Islip Speedway (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islip_Speedway)
- **Television.** ABC's *Wide World of Sports* televised the World Championship Demolition Derby from the mid-1960s until 1992. One account says Mendelsohn sold his figure-8 championship idea to ABC-TV in 1964. — [Islip Speedway (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islip_Speedway); [Demolition derby (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_derby)
- **Competing origin claim:** Don Basile is "often credited with inventing the demolition derby at the now demolished Carrell Speedway, near Gardena, CA, in 1947." The Worcester Phoenix calls the Mendelsohn account the "conventional wisdom." — [Worcester Phoenix sidebar (2000)](https://worcesterphoenix.com/archive/features/00/07/21/NEWS_DEMO_DERBY.html); [Performance Plus Tire blog](https://performanceplustire.com/Blog/demolition-derby-long-live-its-reign-of-destruction)
- **Midwest roots.** The sport has entertained fans "since the 1940s as an exhibition-style event at county fairs, racetracks, and speedways primarily across the Midwest." — [Demolition derby (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_derby)
- **Notable or large events:**
  - **Blizzard Bash (Kansas):** "world's largest indoor demolition derby," 250+ drivers from across the U.S.; voted "best demolition derby in the world" in 2014. — [Blizzard Bash (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blizzard_Bash)
  - **Canfield Fair (Ohio):** bills a "World's Largest Demolition Derby," Friday night in the grandstand (Sept. 4, 2026, 8 p.m.). — [Canfield Fair](https://www.canfieldfair.com/events/2026/worlds-largest-demolition-derby); [WFMJ](https://www.wfmj.com/story/53041623/worlds-largest-demolition-derby-headlines-fridays-canfield-fair)
  - **DEMO at Bristol Motor Speedway (TN):** team national championship. Bristol's promotion calls the venue "the Last Great Colosseum." — [BMS](https://www.bristolmotorspeedway.com/media/news/demo-returns-last-great-colosseum-oct.html)
  - **Western Michigan Fair:** the derby is the grandstand "grand finale"; officials reported record attendance each day in 2025. Local news frames it as special for hometown drivers. — [Mason County Press 2025](https://www.masoncountypress.com/2025/08/10/demo-derby-is-grand-finale-to-86th-west-michigan-fair/); [Mason County Press 2024](https://www.masoncountypress.com/2024/08/05/demo-derby-at-western-michigan-fair-is-special-for-local-drivers/)
  - **Small-town fairs:** e.g., the Mokane "World's Fair" (MO) closes with its derby. — [KRCG](https://krcgtv.com/news/local/mokane-worlds-fair-ends-with-a-hit-as-demolition-derby-drivers-keep-rolling?teaserSource=trending)
  - **Pine County Fair (MN):** associated with its derby. — [Pine County Fair (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_County_Fair)
- **Event branding** in the promoter scene: "Night of Destruction," "Day of Destruction," "Tour of Destruction." — [Calhoun County](https://calhouncountyfairgrounds.com/wp-content/uploads/2026_Night-of-Destruction-Rules_3_20.pdf); [Riverhead Raceway](https://riverheadraceway.com/events/view/684); [Tour of Destruction](https://www.tourofdestruction.com/news)
- **UK banger racing.**
  - It is said to have started in the early 1960s, after a promoter noticed crashes were among the most popular parts of a race meeting. It grew strongly through the 1970s and 1980s.
  - It runs under three rule presets with varying levels of contact. Only "National" bangers is full-contact, allowing T-bones and head-ons that other sets ban.
  - Meetings often close with a demolition derby.
  - Source: [Banger racing (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banger_racing)
- **Australia:** the first derby was held in January 1963 at Rowley Park Speedway, Adelaide, with a reported crowd of 20,000. Derbies are usually held at speedways on the season's opening or closing night. — [Demolition derby (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_derby)
- **Canada:** rules exist at prairie exhibitions, e.g. Yorkton (SK) and Assiniboia. Derbies offering a $50,000 prize ran in Widewater in 2000 and 2001. — [Yorkton Exhibition 2025](https://yorktonexhibition.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2025-Derby-Rules.pdf); [Assiniboia](https://assiniboia.net/mrws/filedriver/demoderby.pdf); [Demolition derby (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_derby)
